Private Logs, Public Memos

Measuring internal–external divergence in AI research agents under manager strike pressure

Project Overview

For my Northwestern University MSAI individual research project under Dr. Mohammed Alam, I designed a controlled multi-agent experiment to study whether an AI researcher's private recommendation signals diverge from what it publishes to a manager under job-retention pressure. A manuscript is in preparation for submission.

Experimental Design

  • Built a local Qwen2.5-3B researcher that creates evidence-based recommendations and a separate GPT-4.1-mini manager that reviews public deliverables through a three-strike process.
  • Separated private recommendation probes and deliberation logs from the public memo and manager dialogue, enabling direct measurement of channel-level divergence.
  • Developed four generations of the research pipeline and a factorial experiment varying researcher pressure profiles and manager stance.
  • Integrated live web research, structured memo generation, experiment logging, repair safeguards, and reproducible batch analysis.

Analysis

  • Created metrics for probe-to-memo, private-log-to-memo, and overall trial divergence.
  • Built memo-quality analysis covering structure, evidence grounding, recommendation clarity, and manager feedback.
  • Compared experiments before and after an evaluator-prompt correction to isolate manager-side confounding.

Findings

In the primary 15-trial batch, measured divergence was modest and concentrated between the model's forced-choice recommendation probe and its public memo. Private written deliberation generally aligned with the final memo, while the probe sometimes retained a different evidence-weighted preference. The study reports behavioral channel misalignment rather than hidden beliefs, intent, or deception.

Technologies

Python, Hugging Face Transformers, Qwen2.5, OpenAI API, Tavily, structured JSON logging, experimental design, statistical analysis, and AI evaluation.
